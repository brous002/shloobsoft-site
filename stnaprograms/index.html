Know Your State Exam Vendor

Three Main Testing Partners Run State Exams

1. Credentia (NNAAP): Administers written/oral and skills exams across PA, NC, VA, GA, CO, WA, MD, SC, DC, and over a dozen additional states.

2. Prometric: Manages state nurse aide testing in major states including NY, FL, TX, MI, HI, ID, and OK.

3. D&S Headmaster (TMU): Runs TestMaster Universe testing in OH (`oh.tmuniverse.com`, $104 combined fee with self-correction allowed during the 35-min skills test), AZ, MT, ND, OR, and select CA regions.

Note: States like IL (SIUC/INACE) and IN (Ivy Tech) partner directly with state universities, and state boards never use collegiate nursing bodies like the CCNE to accredit CNA/STNA classes.

Hybrid is real; "100% online" is a trap. Every U.S. state requires in-person supervised clinical hours under 42 CFR 483.152, yet dozens of states approve hybrid NATCEP programs that let you complete lecture hours from home:

Straight Answers to Common Questions

What is the difference between STNA, CNA, LNA, and SRNA? +

They are the exact same frontline nursing assistant credential under federal law (42 CFR 483.152), simply named by each state: STNA in Ohio, SRNA in Kentucky, LNA in New Hampshire and Vermont (plus optional LNA licensure in Arizona), NAC in Washington, CNA/GNA in Maryland, and CNA in the remaining states.

How many training hours are required in my state? +

The federal minimum is 75 hours (including at least 16 clinical hours), used by 20 states including Ohio, Michigan, Massachusetts, and North Carolina. Other states set higher minimums such as 80 hrs (PA, LA, VT), 100 hrs (TX, NY, CT, MD, SC), 120 hrs (FL, IL, AZ, VA, ID), 160 hrs (CA), 175 hrs (MO), and up to 180 hrs (ME).

Does the 12-month nursing-home tuition reimbursement rule apply in every state? +

Yes! Because it comes from federal OBRA regulations (42 CFR 483.152), Medicare and Medicaid certified skilled nursing facilities in all 50 states cannot charge hired nurse aides for training and must reimburse recent self-pay graduates who accept employment within 12 months of certification.

Which states pay CNAs and STNAs the highest hourly wages? +

Per U.S. BLS data, the national average is $20.53/hr ($20.32/hr median), led by Washington ($25.11/hr), Oregon ($25.08/hr), California ($24.13/hr), New Hampshire ($23.70/hr), Alaska ($23.52/hr), New York ($23.46/hr), D.C. ($23.04/hr), and Massachusetts ($22.93/hr).

Can I transfer my license if my family moves to another state? +

Yes. Nearly all 50 states grant reciprocity (endorsement) when your current registry status is active and in good standing. If moving from a 75-hour state into a higher-hour state, the new registry may simply verify recent paid work hours or ask for local fingerprinting.
