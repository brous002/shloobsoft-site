Official OAC 3701-18 & Headmaster TMU Walkthrough

How to Become a Certified STNA in Ohio (6 Clear Steps)

If state healthcare licensing sounds intimidating from the outside, take a breath. Thousands of first-time Ohio students move from day-one orientation to the state registry every month through these six manageable steps.

Ohio's 75-Hour Training Rule Explained

Under Ohio Administrative Code 3701-18, every state-approved Nurse Aide Training and Competency Evaluation Program (NATCEP) includes at least 75 clock hours (some career centers offer expanded 76 to 90 hour courses adding CPR or Home Health skills) across three supportive phases:

Phase 1: Core Pre-Clinical FoundationsFirst 16 Hours

Completed before any direct resident care: infection control, handwashing, communication, safety, resident independence, and resident rights.

Phase 2: Classroom & Hands-On Skills Lab43+ Hours (59 Total Class/Lab)

Practice vital signs, range-of-motion exercises, gait-belt transfers, positioning, bathing, and nutrition in a relaxed lab with classmates first.

Phase 3: Supervised Clinical ExperienceMinimum 16 Hours

Care for real residents in a licensed Ohio long-term care community right alongside your RN instructor.

2026 Fact Check

Two Outdated Myths You May See on Older Websites

Myth 1 ("Ohio requires 100 hours"): Ohio's legal minimum is 75 hours, not 100. Career centers like Lorain County JVS or Polaris offer 90-hour tracks that bundle extra CPR or Home Health Aide skills, while 75 hours fully qualifies you for the state exam.

Myth 2 ("Look for CCNE accreditation"): The CCNE accredits 4-year bachelor's and graduate nursing degrees, never STNA classes. In Ohio, simply confirm that your school holds an active Ohio Department of Health (ODH) NATCEP approval number.

1

Simple Entry Checks

Most adult cohorts ask for a photo ID, an Ohio BCI fingerprint check, and a negative TB test (2-step skin test or blood screen). Better yet, Ohio NATCEP rules do not require a completed high school diploma or GED to take the state STNA exam.

2

Pick Your ODH-Approved Class

Compare county career centers, community colleges, hybrid courses, or $0 nursing-facility cohorts. Tip: check whether books and your $104 state exam fee are bundled in tuition (schools like Great Oaks and Apollo bundle them automatically).

3

Finish Lab & Guided Clinicals

Because every hour counts in a 75-hour course, pick a daytime, evening, or weekend schedule you can attend comfortably. On your final day, your school uploads your completion record straight into Ohio's TMU exam portal for you.

4

Pick Your Test Date in TMU

Ohio partners with D&S Diversified Technologies (Headmaster) via oh.tmuniverse.com. You can schedule your Knowledge and Skills tests together on the same day or book them separately, often right inside the school lab where you trained.

5

Pass Knowledge & Skills ($104)

Knowledge ($26 written / $36 audio): 79 multiple-choice questions in 90 minutes (70% passes). Skills ($78): 3 or 4 tasks in 35 minutes (task 1 embeds soap-and-water handwashing). Reassuring perk: Ohio lets you self-correct any step before calling time!

6

Keep Your Registry Active

Once passed, you join the official Ohio Nurse Aide Registry. Under OAC 3701-17-07.3, working just 7.5 consecutive hours (or 8 hours within a 48-hour period) of paid nursing-related care every 24 consecutive months keeps you active with zero re-testing.

ODH Hybrid Rules

Can You Take STNA Classes Online in Ohio?

Yes, in a hybrid format! While no state permits a 100% online nursing assistant license (because safe bedside care takes real hands-on practice), Ohio approves hybrid NATCEP programs that let you study theory from your couch:

  • 1. Online Classroom Modules (At Home, 24/7): Watch skill videos, study infection control and resident care, and complete chapter check-ins from your laptop or phone after work or once the kids are asleep.
  • 2. In-Person Skills Lab Coaching: Meet your RN instructor on campus to practice blood pressure, gait-belt transfers, and all testable Headmaster tasks until they feel second nature.
  • 3. 16-Hour Local Clinical Rotation: Complete at least 16 supervised clinical hours (often two weekend shifts or four evening shifts) at a partnered Ohio senior-care community.
Friendly Heads-Up: Skip any out-of-state website selling an "instant 100% online CNA certificate" without an Ohio clinical site. Only schools holding an active ODH NATCEP number can qualify you in Ohio's TMU exam portal.

Straight Answers to Common Questions

How many hours does Ohio require for STNA training? +

Ohio Administrative Code 3701-18 sets a minimum of 75 total hours in an ODH-approved program: at least 59 hours of classroom and lab instruction (with 16 core hours completed before resident contact) plus at least 16 hours of supervised clinical care in a long-term care community.

Who runs the Ohio STNA test and how much are the exam fees? +

D&S Diversified Technologies (Headmaster) runs Ohio testing through TestMaster Universe (oh.tmuniverse.com). The 79-question Knowledge exam is $26 ($36 for the audio read-aloud option) and the 35-minute Skills exam (3 or 4 tasks) is $78, totaling $104 ($114 audio). You can schedule both parts together or separately.

What if I miss a step during the 35-minute Skills test? +

Here is one of the best confidence boosters in Ohio's Headmaster exam rules: if you realize you forgot a step (like locking a brake or opening a privacy curtain), you can say so and go back to perform or correct any step on any task at any point before your 35 minutes end and before telling the observer you are finished.

Can I get hired into free training, or get my tuition reimbursed? +

Yes! Many Ohio skilled nursing communities hire beginners directly into $0 paid training cohorts. Even if you pay out of pocket at a local school first, federal OBRA and Ohio rules entitle you to tuition and state exam reimbursement if you join a Medicare/Medicaid-certified nursing facility within 12 months of certification.

Do I need a high school diploma or GED before starting? +

Ohio's state NATCEP rules do not require a completed high school diploma to take the STNA exam, allowing high school students and adult learners finishing their GED (including Ohio's tuition-free Adult Diploma 18+ pathways at career centers) to get certified first.

How do I keep my Ohio STNA certification from expiring? +

Under OAC 3701-17-07.3, as long as you perform at least 7.5 consecutive hours (or 8 hours within a 48-hour period) of paid nursing or nursing-related duties at least once every 24 consecutive months, your registry listing stays active without re-testing.
